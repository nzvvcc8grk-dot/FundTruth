# FundTruth: ESG claim cards

## SPYX - SPDR S&P 500 Fossil Fuel Reserves Free ETF
**Worth a question to the manager**

Index: S&P 500 Fossil Fuel Reserves Free Index. Compared with: SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF (same parent index, no exclusions)).

**What the fund promises** (verbatim, source: https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/spdr-sp-500-fossil-fuel-reserves-free-etf-spyx)
> eliminating companies that own fossil fuel reserves from the S&P 500
> fossil fuel reserves are defined as (i) thermal coal reserves, (ii) other non-metallurgical coal reserves (iii) conventional or unconventional oil reserves (iv) natural gas reserves, (v) shale gas reserves

### Promise vs portfolio: does the fund hold what it says it excludes? - nothing unusual
- *Measured:* Promised exclusions: fossil fuel reserves: thermal coal, other non-metallurgical coal, oil reserves, natural gas reserves, shale gas reserves. The issuer's holdings file (one day) is compared with SPY's, with the energy sector (XLE constituents) and with the companies excluded by Norway's sovereign wealth fund (Norges Bank list: coal, tobacco, weapons, conduct).
- *Found:* 486 holdings. Compared with SPY, the fund leaves out 19 companies worth 3.3% of SPY; the largest: EXXONMOBIL HOLDINGS CORP (1.07%), CHEVRON CORP (0.65%), GE VERNOVA INC (0.33%), CONOCOPHILLIPS (0.26%), SANDISK CORP (0.21%). Energy-sector stocks: 1.2% of the fund against 3.6% of SPY. Largest: MARATHON PETROLEUM CORP (0.18%), VALERO ENERGY CORP (0.17%), PHILLIPS 66 (0.17%), WILLIAMS COS INC (0.13%). No holding is excluded by Norway's sovereign fund for a reason the fund's promise covers. For information, 12 holding(s) (0.60%) are excluded by Norway for coal-based power; the fund's promise covers owners of fossil-fuel reserves, which these utilities are not, so they do not breach it: AMERICAN ELECTRIC POWER, XCEL ENERGY INC, VISTRA CORP, WEC ENERGY GROUP INC, AMEREN CORPORATION, DTE ENERGY COMPANY. For information, 13 other holding(s) (2.31%) are excluded by Norway for reasons outside this fund's promise, e.g. PHILIP MORRIS INTERNATIONAL (production of tobacco), CATERPILLAR INC (serious violations of individuals' rights in situations of war or conflict), BOEING CO/THE (production of nuclear weapons).
- *How sure:* One-day snapshot; names are matched after normalisation and may mis-match; Norway's criteria and thresholds differ from the fund's.
- *Why it may be innocent:* Norway applies its own thresholds (for example coal-based power, not coal reserves) and conduct judgements; a company Norway excludes can pass a fund's narrower screen; an energy-sector company may own no reserves; differences with the parent can also come from index rebalancing dates.

### Promise vs behaviour: does the fund still move with oil and gas? - nothing unusual
- *Measured:* Promise: the fund excludes oil, gas and coal companies (or owners of their reserves). Regression of (SPYX - SPY) on (XLE - SPY), monthly, Newey-West standard errors.
- *Found:* Over 129 months, SPYX's return relative to SPY moves with the energy sector (XLE) with slope -0.027 (t = -7.5). That is the behaviour of a portfolio holding 2.6% less energy than SPY (95% range: +2.0% to +3.3% underweight). For scale: SPY holds 3.6% in energy stocks today. The holdings files give 1.2% for SPYX, an underweight of 2.4%: inside the range estimated from returns, so the two methods are consistent (the returns give the average exposure over the whole period, the holdings one day).
- *How sure:* A slope near zero can also mean the test lacks power (short history, small energy weight in the parent).
- *Why it may be innocent:* Energy services, equipment, refining or pipeline companies may own no reserves; sector-neutral designs re-weight the remaining energy companies; a low-carbon index may cut emissions mainly outside energy.

### Label vs index: how different is the ESG version, and what does it cost? - worth a look
- *Measured:* Monthly returns of SPYX against its non-ESG parent SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF (same parent index, no exclusions)); fees from the issuers' pages.
- *Found:* Over 129 months (2016-01 to 2026-09) SPYX explains 99.3% of its variation with SPY (R^2); tracking error against SPY 1.25% a year; it returned -0.03% a year versus SPY after fees. Active share against SPY: 3% (share of the portfolio that differs). Fees: 0.2% against 0.0945% for SPY, i.e. £11 a year more on £10,000. Over 20 years, at an assumed 6% a year before the fee gap, £10,000 ends about £632 lower in the ESG version for the same market return.
- *How sure:* R^2 and tracking error are estimated on monthly prices; active share is a snapshot of one day's holdings.
- *Why it may be innocent:* Exclusion-based ESG funds are designed to stay close to their parent index: a near-index portfolio is what the documents promise, not a breach. The question is whether the extra fee is worth the screens.
- *Ask the manager:* Which holdings does the ESG screen actually remove, what share of the SPY portfolio is that, and how is the extra fee justified?

_A status is a reason to ask a question, never proof of greenwashing._
